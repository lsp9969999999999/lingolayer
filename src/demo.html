The Quiet Revolution in Everyday Software

For most of the past decade, software improved in ways users could see: brighter interfaces, faster page loads, smoother animations. The changes arriving now are harder to notice but more consequential, because they alter what the software understands rather than how it looks.

Translation is a good example. A tool that merely swaps words produces text that is technically correct and practically unreadable. A tool that grasps context can keep the original meaning, tone, and rhythm intact, which is the difference between decoding a sentence and actually reading it.

The best interface is the one that disappears once you stop noticing it.
